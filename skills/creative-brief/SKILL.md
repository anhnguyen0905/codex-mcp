# Creative Brief (what must be fixed before creative work starts)

## The brief fixes decisions, not craft

A brief locks what creatives must not have to guess and leaves everything else open. Prescribe the
execution and it is a script; leave the message open and it is a wish list. Nine things must be
settled before anyone opens a design file:

```
1 Objective         the business change wanted + the KPI that proves it moved
2 Audience          who, behaviourally — not demographics alone
3 Insight           the tension in that audience's life the work speaks to
4 Single message    ONE proposition, one sentence, per asset
5 Reason to believe the evidence that makes the message credible
6 Mandatories       logo, legal, claims, disclaimers, brand and platform rules
7 Deliverables      exact list: format, ratio, duration, language, placement, master + cutdowns
8 Timeline          briefing, review dates, capped revision rounds, final handover
9 Budget + approval production budget, one named decision-maker, sign-off order
```

Everything else is the creative team's territory: a brief that also supplies the headline, palette
and shot list has replaced the work it commissioned. The brief states problem and constraints; the
script or storyboard is one proposed answer to it, authored by the creative side and judged against
the brief. Keep them in separate documents — merged, every review argues execution and the strategy
is never tested.

## One message per asset

Messages compete for the same few seconds. Two propositions in one film do not double recall; they
split it and usually leave neither. Five stakeholder messages means five assets or a ranked funnel
sequence, not one crowded film. Write it as a plain declarative sentence a stranger could repeat
after one viewing.

## Writing the insight

An insight is not a fact, a feature, or a restatement of the target — it is a tension the product can
resolve. Test it: does the audience recognise itself, and does the product genuinely answer it?
Source from research, reviews, support tickets, playtests or sales calls, and cite each source.

```
Fact:        "Players churn at level 12."
Observation: "Level 12 takes four evenings to clear."
Insight:     "Players want to feel they're progressing in the twenty minutes a night they
              actually have — a wall makes them feel demoted for having a job."
```

## Feedback: actionable vs taste-based

Actionable feedback names the brief clause the work misses and leaves the fix to the creative team.
Consolidate all reviewers into one written response per round, resolve internal disagreement before
sending, and never route contradictory notes to the agency to arbitrate.

```
Taste-based: "I don't like the blue." · "Make it pop." · "Try something bolder."
Actionable:  "Single message is speed, but the first three seconds show the roster, so the
              proposition doesn't land before the scroll." (clause 4)
             "Legal needs the disclaimer on screen 2s; it's currently 0.5s." (clause 6)
```

## Failure modes

- **Brief by committee**: each stakeholder adds a line until objective, audience and message broaden
  to describe everyone and instruct no one.
- **No single decision-maker**: approved by the loudest voice, or re-litigated after final sign-off.
- **Message stuffing**: features, awards, price, purpose and a CTA in one 15s cut.
- **KPI-less objective**: "raise awareness" with no measure, so nobody can tell if it worked.
- **Missing deliverable specs**: assets arrive in the wrong ratio or duration for placements that
  were always in the media plan, and the reshoot lands on the brief author.
- **Insight replaced by a targeting line** ("males 18-34 who like shooters").
- **No review dates**: the first sighting of the work is the day it must ship.
- **Mandatories surfacing after production**: legal, claims or platform rules discovered late.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Objective states a business change plus the KPI that measures it
- [ ] Audience defined behaviourally; insight is a tension with its source cited
- [ ] Exactly one message per asset, as one plain sentence; reason to believe substantiated
- [ ] Mandatories complete and confirmed before production starts
- [ ] Deliverables specify format, ratio, duration, language and placement
- [ ] Timeline names review dates and caps revision rounds; budget stated
- [ ] One named decision-maker and a written approval order
- [ ] No execution prescriptions in the brief; script kept in a separate document
- [ ] Every feedback note cites a brief clause, not a preference

## Provenance

Standard advertising briefing practice (single-minded proposition, insight-led briefing, separating
brief from execution, consolidated single-voice feedback). The nine-element list, insight test and
failure-mode set are **derived, unverified** working conventions, not a citable standard. Any numeric
limit taken from here — revision-round caps, asset durations, disclaimer on-screen time — must be
replaced with the project's own measured values and its legal team's actual requirements before being
treated as a target.
