# Influencer Strategy (creator selection, contracting, measurement)

## Tiers are roles, not sizes

```
nano  (~1k-10k)    cheapest per post, high comment intimacy; local/niche proof and cheap volume
                   testing of angles. Most ops overhead per unit of reach.
micro (~10k-100k)  workhorse: real topical authority, negotiable, enough volume to A/B creators.
mid   (~100k-500k) meaningful single-post reach with retained community trust; anchors a wave.
macro/celebrity    reach, credibility transfer, PR value. Priced like media, not word of mouth;
                   low CPE, weaker comment-level trust, key-person risk.
```

Bands differ by platform and market — re-cut them from your own data; the tier's job is the point.
Portfolios beat single tiers: macro/mid for reach and legitimacy, micro/nano for coverage,
repeatability and cheap creative learning.

## Select on fit, not follower count

Rank on audience overlap with the target (geo, language, age, interest — from the creator's own
audience export, not a guess) · topical authenticity (do they use the category unpaid?) · comment
substance and sentiment · brand-deal density, since back-to-back sponsorships convert poorly ·
brand-safety history. Follower count only sets the price band; an audience 70% outside your market is
expensive at any CPM.

## CPM / CPE as planning inputs

```
CPM = fee / (expected impressions / 1000)
CPE = fee / expected engagements   ← define engagement: likes+comments+shares+saves
CPV = fee / expected views         ← state the platform's view definition; they differ
expected impressions ≈ MEDIAN views of the creator's last N comparable organic posts
```

Use recent medians, never peaks and never follower count. Sanity-check the resulting CPM against your
paid CPM for the same placement. These are estimates: pay on deliverables and terms, and negotiate a
make-good clause if delivered reach lands far below the stated median.

## Brief, creative freedom, and contract terms

Give only the non-negotiables — single message, mandatory and prohibited claims, hook window, CTA and
link/code, delivery spec. The creator owns format, voice, script and edit; that native voice is what
you are buying, and word-for-word scripting reads as an ad and loses the trust you paid for. Approve a
concept or outline instead of line-editing. The contract must then fix:

- **Usage rights** — what you may reuse, where (organic repost, paid amplification, owned channels,
  OOH), for how long, which markets. Whitelisting (ads run from the creator's handle) must be named
  explicitly and priced separately from the post.
- **Exclusivity** — category definition, duration, and whether competitor mentions are barred too.
  Broad, long exclusivity is expensive and must be paid for.
- **Disclosure** — paid partnership disclosed clearly and prominently per each market's rules (e.g.
  #ad plus the platform's paid-partnership label), as a contractual obligation, verified on the live
  post: the advertiser generally carries the regulatory risk.
- **Deliverables** — asset count, placement, live date, minimum live duration, approval window,
  revision rights, deletion rules; plus kill fee, payment terms, prohibited adjacencies, AI-content
  rules, and required insights reporting with a deadline.

## Measurement, and what platform reach cannot prove

Instrument everything: a unique promo code per creator, unique deep links with distinct campaign
parameters, per-creator landing pages where volume justifies it, and a post-purchase "where did you
hear about us?" question to catch untracked influence. Reach, views and engagements are exposure
metrics and cannot establish incrementality — a creator's audience is already predisposed to the
category, so codes and last-click over-credit the creator while brand effects go uncounted. For a real
read use a holdout: matched geos with and without creator activity, or audience-level holdouts where
the platform allows, measuring the delta in installs, sales or brand metrics. Report tracked and
incremental results as two separate numbers.

## Fraud screening, and repeat vs one-off

Screen before paying: engagement rate far above or below the tier norm (very high ER with generic
one-word comments suggests pods or bought engagement) · follower growth in step changes rather than a
curve · follower geography inconsistent with content language · many followers with no posts or
default avatars · views wildly out of line with followers in either direction · reach shown only as
uncheckable screenshots. Each is grounds to demand the platform's audience export, not an auto-reject.

One-offs are discovery: test many creators and angles cheaply, keep the winners. Repeat and ambassador
deals compound — sustained visible use rather than a single paid mention, better rates for commitment,
lower briefing overhead. Fund a discovery pool plus a retained core, and let measured performance
decide who is retained.

## Failure modes

- Buying follower count, then finding the audience is in the wrong market or age band.
- Treating code redemptions or last-click as incremental lift.
- Scripting the creator until the content sounds like a TV ad.
- No usage rights, so the best-performing asset cannot be amplified as paid media.
- Disclosure left to the creator's discretion, leaving the advertiser exposed.
- Comparing creator CPM to paid CPM without reconciling view definitions.
- Judging the channel on one post; creator variance is high, so judge at portfolio level.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] Each creator justified by audience-fit evidence from their own audience data
- [ ] Expected impressions from recent median organic performance, not peaks or followers
- [ ] CPM/CPE quoted with the engagement and view definitions written out
- [ ] Brief carries message and mandatories only; execution left to the creator
- [ ] Contract covers usage rights, whitelisting, exclusivity scope, disclosure, deletion rights
- [ ] Disclosure verified on the live post against each market's rules
- [ ] Unique codes, unique links and a survey question all in place
- [ ] Incrementality claims backed by a holdout; platform reach labelled exposure only
- [ ] Fraud screen documented per paid creator; repeat-vs-one-off tied to measured performance
