# SME Operations (pricing, break-even, cash, KPIs)

## Cash first — profit second

SMEs die of cash, not of losses. A profitable business with 60-day receivables and 30-day payables
funds its own growth out of pocket and can fail while the P&L looks fine. Every operational
decision (pricing, terms, inventory, hiring) gets checked against the cash forecast before the
margin math.

```
runway (months) = cash on hand / average monthly net cash burn
cash conversion cycle = days inventory + days receivable − days payable
```

A 13-week rolling cash forecast is the core instrument: weekly buckets, actual opening balance,
committed inflows (invoices by expected — not due — date), committed outflows (payroll, rent, tax,
suppliers), updated weekly against actuals. Taxes (VAT, payroll withholding) are the classic
omission: cash that sits in the account but is already owed.

## Pricing and margin structure

```
gross margin % = (price − direct cost) / price          ← margin is on PRICE
markup %       = (price − direct cost) / direct cost    ← markup is on COST
price for target margin m: price = direct cost / (1 − m)
```

Confusing markup with margin understates price systematically (50% markup = 33% margin). Direct
cost must be fully loaded: materials, direct labor at true hourly cost (wages + taxes + benefits),
payment fees, shipping, returns/spoilage. Cost-plus is the floor; price to value where the market
allows, and test — SMEs almost universally underprice, and a price rise loses less volume than
owners fear. Rule of thumb: a business with healthy demand and zero complaints about price is
priced too low.

## Break-even

```
contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost per unit
break-even units   = fixed costs / contribution margin per unit
break-even revenue = fixed costs / contribution margin %
```

Classify costs honestly: rent, salaries, insurance, software = fixed; materials, transaction fees,
commissions, freight = variable. Semi-fixed steps (the next hire, the next machine) create a new
break-even — recompute at each step, don't average across it. Use break-even for decisions: new
hire = added fixed cost ÷ contribution margin = extra units needed; can the pipeline deliver that?

## Working capital, inventory, terms

- **Terms are a price**: 2/10 net 30 (2% discount for paying in 10 days) is ~36% annualized — take
  it if cash allows, offer it only knowingly.
- Negotiate asymmetry in your favor: collect faster (deposits, milestone billing, card on file)
  and pay slower (net terms with suppliers) — every day shaved off the cash conversion cycle is
  free financing.
- Inventory is cash in a box: order to demand with a small safety stock, watch turns
  (COGS / average inventory), and clear dead stock at a loss rather than warehousing it forever —
  the cash and space are worth more than the pride.

## The KPI dashboard — one page, weekly

Pick 6–10, no more: cash on hand + weeks of runway, sales/bookings vs same week last year,
gross margin %, AR overdue (amount + oldest), pipeline or bookings forward, and 1–3 operational
drivers specific to the business (utilization, orders shipped on time, table turns). Each KPI needs
an owner and a threshold that triggers action. A dashboard nobody acts on is decoration.

## Seasonality and owner decisions

Plan on last year's monthly shape, not the annual average: build cash in peak months to cover
troughs, secure a credit line **before** it's needed (banks lend to businesses that don't need it),
and time inventory/hiring to the season, not the calendar year. Owner-operator decision hygiene:
put a price on your own hour and delegate everything below it; for each recurring fire, fix the
system, not the instance; and make reversible decisions fast, irreversible ones (leases, key hires,
large inventory buys) against the downside cash scenario, not the forecast.

## Failure modes

- Growth eating cash: sales up, receivables and inventory up faster, payroll missed in month nine.
- Pricing off markup-vs-margin confusion, or off incomplete direct cost (owner labor at zero).
- One customer >30–40% of revenue treated as an asset instead of a named risk.
- Tax cash (VAT, withholding) spent because it was "in the account".
- Discounting to hit revenue targets while contribution margin goes negative on discounted units.
- Fixed-cost commitments (lease, hires) made against peak-season revenue as if it were year-round.
- Twenty KPIs, none with an owner or a trigger.

## Reviewer checklist

- [ ] 13-week cash forecast exists, updated against actuals, taxes included
- [ ] Runway and cash conversion cycle stated in numbers
- [ ] Margin vs markup used correctly; direct costs fully loaded (owner labor priced)
- [ ] Break-even computed with honest fixed/variable split, recomputed at step costs
- [ ] Receivable/payable terms stated and priced (early-pay discounts annualized)
- [ ] Dashboard ≤10 KPIs, each with owner + action threshold
- [ ] Customer concentration and downside season scenario named

## Provenance

Standard small-business operating practice. The specific figures (13 weeks, 6–10 KPIs, 30–40%
concentration, the 2/10-net-30 annualization) are rules of thumb and conventions — calibrate to
the business's actual cycle, industry norms, and jurisdiction before acting.
